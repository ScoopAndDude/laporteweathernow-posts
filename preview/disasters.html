EARTH FROM SPACE

The whole planet, right now.

Two NOAA weather satellites hang 22,236 miles above the equator, each watching its side of the Earth. These are their newest pictures of the whole planet, taken every 10 minutes. Hurricanes, big storm systems and wildfire smoke all show up here.

Explore NASA Worldview ↗

Pictures: NOAA/NESDIS GOES-East and GOES-West GeoColor (public domain). On the night side you see city lights and cloud tops from infrared, not true color. The satellite map uses NASA’s most recent full day of pictures (VIIRS, through NASA GIBS). Fires: every fire NASA’s VIIRS cameras spotted in the last 24 hours, from NASA FIRMS, refreshed every 3 hours. Spots that showed heat on 5 or more of the last 7 days are marked in gray as steady heat sources, since those are almost always steel mills, refineries, gas flares or volcanoes. Weather satellites: worked out live in your browser from CelesTrak’s orbits, good to within a few miles.

    Weather services around the world

    Official warnings come from each country's own weather service. Start with the WMO map, which shows them all in one place.

    • WMO Severe Weather Information Centre ↗Official warnings from national weather services worldwide, on one map
    • National Hurricane Center (NOAA) ↗Atlantic and eastern Pacific hurricanes
    • Joint Typhoon Warning Center ↗Typhoons and cyclones in the Pacific and Indian Oceans
    • ECMWF forecast charts ↗The European model, one of the world's most accurate
    • Meteoalarm ↗Official weather warnings for all of Europe
    • Japan Meteorological Agency ↗Typhoons, earthquakes and tsunami warnings for Japan
    • India Meteorological Department ↗Monsoon and cyclones in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea
    • Australia Bureau of Meteorology ↗Cyclones, fire weather and its own El Niño outlook
    • Environment Canada ↗Warnings for our neighbors to the north
    • UK Met Office ↗Forecasts and warnings for the United Kingdom

    Data: GDACS (a cooperation of the United Nations and the European Commission) and the U.S. Geological Survey. La Porte Weather Now is not affiliated with either and does not issue warnings.
