    WHY IT MATTERS

    What the Amazon really does, and one myth

    Myth

    “The lungs of the planet”

    You may have heard the Amazon makes 20% of the world’s oxygen. Scientists estimate its plants make about 6 to 9%, and the forest’s own trees and microbes use nearly all of it, so its net share is close to zero.

    Source: FactCheck.org ↗

    Fact

    It holds a huge amount of carbon

    The Amazon stores about 150 to 200 billion metric tons of carbon in its trees and soil. If it all went into the air, carbon dioxide would rise by about 100 parts per million, roughly a quarter more than today.

    Source: FactCheck.org ↗

    Fact

    It makes its own rain

    About 30% of the moisture over the Amazon comes from the forest itself, as trees breathe out water that falls again as rain. A 2026 study found clearing caused most of the drop in rainfall in the southern Amazon since 1980.

    Source: Mongabay, on a Nature Communications study ↗

    Close to home

    The same El Niño

    This year’s strong El Niño links us: the same event that usually brings milder winters to the northern U.S. is expected to bring heat and drought to the Amazon this fall.

    More: our El Niño watch

    WHO’S PROTECTING IT

    The people on the front line

    Peru · Madre de Dios

    Paul Rosolie and Junglekeepers

    Paul Rosolie, a conservationist and author, leads Junglekeepers, whose local rangers protect rainforest along Peru’s Las Piedras River. In January 2026, after an armed ambush on conservation leaders and attacks from narcos, illegal miners and loggers, they launched END GAME: 18 months to protect 50,000 acres of the Las Piedras corridor and make it a national park. His book Junglekeeper came out in 2026.

    Support Junglekeepers ↗   About END GAME ↗
    Brazil · Xingu

    Juma Xipaia

    Juma Xipaia is a leader of the Xipaya people in Brazil’s Amazon. She became the first woman chief in the Middle Xingu region and later Brazil’s first secretary of Indigenous rights. She has survived six assassination attempts while standing up to illegal gold mining that poisons rivers with mercury, to deforestation and to land invasion. Her story is the documentary Yanuni, shortlisted for the 2026 Academy Awards.

    HOW TO HELP

    What you can do from here

    Trusted groups working on the ground, free tools to watch the forest yourself, and everyday choices that add up. We don’t get anything for these links.

    Protect the forest directly

    • Junglekeepers ↗ Local rangers guarding the Las Piedras River forest in Peru (Paul Rosolie’s group).
    • Rainforest Trust ↗ Buys and protects threatened rainforest with local partners.
    • Amazon Conservation ↗ Protects land in Peru and Bolivia and runs MAAP, the satellite watch on clearing and gold mining.
    • Re:wild ↗ Protects wild places and searches for species feared lost.
    • Big international groups working there too: WWF ↗, Conservation International ↗, The Nature Conservancy ↗ and Rainforest Action Network ↗.

    Stand with Indigenous peoples

    • Rainforest Foundation US ↗ Helps Indigenous communities secure their land and patrol it with phones, drones and satellite alerts.
    • Amazon Frontlines ↗ Backs Indigenous peoples defending their land in Ecuador and Peru.
    • Amazon Watch ↗ A nonprofit (not us) that works with Indigenous groups to protect the Amazon.
    • COICA ↗ The alliance of Indigenous peoples across the whole Amazon basin.
    • APIB ↗ Brazil’s national alliance of Indigenous peoples.
    • Survival International ↗ Speaks up for tribal and uncontacted peoples.

    Watch the forest yourself, free

    • Global Forest Watch ↗ Near-live maps of tree loss and fire alerts anywhere on Earth.
    • MAAP ↗ Satellite reports on new clearing and gold mining in the Andes and Amazon.
    • NASA FIRMS ↗ NASA’s fire map, updated through the day.
    • INPE TerraBrasilis ↗ Brazil’s official deforestation numbers.
    • Imazon ↗ Brazilian researchers who track clearing month by month.

    Everyday choices

    • Buying gold? Ask for recycled or Fairmined ↗ gold, so none of it comes from illegal mines that poison Amazon rivers with mercury.
    • Look for FSC ↗ on wood and paper, and the Rainforest Alliance ↗ frog seal on coffee, cocoa and bananas.
    • Before you give, look the charity up on Charity Navigator ↗.
    • Watch Yanuni, check the numbers here each week, and share this page. People protect what they know about.
