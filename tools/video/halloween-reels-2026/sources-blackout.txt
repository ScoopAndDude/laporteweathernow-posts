FACT-CHECK: "Could your house last 3 days?" (Parts 1-3)
Bottom line: nothing is flatly wrong. A few lines overreach, the 90-day figure has only one source, and some pictures could mislead.

(1) VERDICTS

PART 1
1. "Fox News reports the Pentagon wants major bases able to run 90 days on their own" / "As reported by Fox News" / "Fox News (Oct. 25, 2026)": SUPPORTED as attribution; the figure itself has only one source. FOX: major continental U.S. installations "would be expected to sustain essential operations independently for at least 90 days," per "documents reviewed by Fox News Digital." IBT, citing Fox: "a reported requirement." It is not in the speech (REV), and ASF reported no duration target.
2. "THE PENTAGON'S 90-DAY PLAN": OVERSTATED: it is a reported goal, and it reads like "a plan lasting 90 days."
3. "FORTRESS America, announced Sept. 30, 2026 by Pete Hegseth": SUPPORTED. DS: announced in the Sept. 30, 2026 "State of the Force" address at Marine Corps Base Quantico. DW: "Today, at my direction, we are launching FORTRESS America." ASF agrees.
4. "Bases that make their own power": SUPPORTED. REV and ASF: "every major military installation in America is going to generate its own power, independent of the civilian grid."
5. "our bases stay lit": SUPPORTED, as the end of a longer sentence. DS: "If an adversary takes down the grid — by cyberattack, by sabotage, or by any means — our bases stay lit." DW and REV match.
6. "Not a blackout notice / nothing in it schedules power cuts for homes": SUPPORTED: no source (FOX, DS, ASF, DW, REV) mentions homes or scheduled cuts. IMPRECISE: "nothing in it" vouches for documents only Fox has seen.
7. "A long-term plan for military bases": MOSTLY SUPPORTED. ASF: full achievement "could take decades." But FORTRESS has five pillars, including supply chains and space, not only bases.
8-10. Flashlights not candles (fire risk) / unplug electronics (surges) / keep the fridge and freezer shut: SUPPORTED. CDCPO: flashlights "rather than candles... to minimize the risk of fire." FEMA: "Unplug appliances, equipment and electronics to avoid damage from electrical surges." READY: "Keep freezers and refrigerators closed." RC: "not candles"; "surges or spikes can damage equipment"; doors "closed as much as possible."
11. "NIPSCO: text OUT to 444111, or NIPSCO.com": SUPPORTED. NIP: text "OUT" to 444111, or "Report Outage" online; NWIB agrees. LOCAL NITPICK: POUS counts LaPorte County customers as NIPSCO 92,059, Indiana Michigan Power 6,354 and Kankakee Valley REMC 2,674. HTN: I&M outages in Springville, Hudson Lake and Rolling Prairie. FE: KV REMC is "headquartered in Wanatah." The script labels the number as NIPSCO's but gives other customers nothing.
12. "Check on neighbors, if it's safe": SUPPORTED. FEMA: "Check on family members and neighbors if it is safe to do so." READY agrees.

PART 2
13. "Outside only, at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents": SUPPORTED. CDCPSA: "Keep generators outside, at least 20 feet from doors, windows, and vents." IDHS agrees. CPSC: "outside only, at least 20 feet away from the house."
14. "Never in the garage, even with the door open": SUPPORTED. CDCPO: "even if the doors or windows are open." CPSC: "Opening doors or windows will not provide enough ventilation."
15-18. Can't see or smell it / batteries or battery backup / no gas oven or stove for heat / no grills indoors: SUPPORTED. CPSC: "colorless and odorless"; "battery-operated alarms or alarms with battery backup." CDCPO: "battery-powered or battery backup CO detector"; "Never use a gas range or oven to heat a home"; never use "a charcoal grill... inside a home." NWS and IDHS agree on ovens and grills.
19. CO signs (headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, confusion): SUPPORTED but partial. CDCCO and IDHS: "headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion." CDCCO: sleeping or drunk people "can die... before they have symptoms."
20. "Alarm or symptoms? Fresh air, then 911": SUPPORTED. CPSC: "Get outside immediately and call 911." IDHS: exit "immediately after a CO alarm goes off" and call "emergency personnel."
21. "Loose layers, a hat, blankets / close off rooms": SUPPORTED. NWS: "closing off rooms you do not need, and dressing in layers and wearing a cap." FEMAW: "Close off unused rooms." CDCPO: "Extra blankets."
22. "Go somewhere with heat, like a friend's or a warming center": SUPPORTED. CDCPO: "seek shelter with friends or at a community shelter." FEMA: "go to an alternate location for heat."
23. "In Indiana, call 2-1-1 to find a warming center": SUPPORTED. FSSA: "call 211 or 866-211-9966 to find their nearest warming center." WTHI agrees.

PART 3
24-25. Fridge about 4 hours; freezer 48 hours full, 24 half full: SUPPORTED. FSG: "about 4 hours if the door is kept closed"; "about 48 hours, or 24 hours if it is half-full." FDAF and CDCF agree.
26. "Appliance thermometer in each; fridge 40°F or colder": SUPPORTED in the narration. FDAF: "appliance thermometers in your refrigerator and freezer"; "refrigerator is at or below 40° F." IMPRECISE on screen: "40°F or colder" sits under "fridge and freezer," but freezers belong "at or below 0° F" (FDAF).
27. "Power out more than 4 hours? Toss perishables": IMPRECISE. Every source says refrigerated food. CDCF: "After 4 hours without power, throw out refrigerated foods like meat, fish, eggs, milk and leftovers." FSG: freezer food that "still contains ice crystals or is 40°F... or below" can be refrozen.
28. "Don't taste-test; when in doubt, throw it out": SUPPORTED. CDCF: "Never taste food to determine if it is safe to eat... When in doubt, throw it out." FSG agrees.
29. "1 gallon a person, a day, at least 3 days": SUPPORTED. CDCW: "at least 1 gallon of water per person, per day for 3 days"; "Try to store a 2-week supply if possible." RCKIT: "3-day supply for evacuation, 2-week supply for home."
30. "A family of 4 needs 12 gallons": the math is right (4 x 1 x 3 = 12), but "needs" is IMPRECISE because 12 is a minimum. CDCW: store more for pets, sickness, pregnancy and heat.
31. "Ask your pharmacist now": SUPPORTED. FDAD: "contact a pharmacist, health care provider, or the manufacturer." FEMA: "Make a plan for refrigerating medicines."
