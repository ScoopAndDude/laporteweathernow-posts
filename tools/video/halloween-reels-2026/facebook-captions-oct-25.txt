2) posey-chapel-cemetery-the-bells.mp4

Near Hesston, there's a country cemetery where people say they hear bells 🔔 Posey Chapel Cemetery dates to 1833, when it began as a family plot. Its last chapel, built in 1855, was destroyed by arson in 1972. But legend tells of an older fire, set by a deranged preacher. Visitors report a woman in white, singing, and lights that bounce between the graves. Please visit in daylight and obey posted hours. Double-tap ❤️ for the next La Porte legend.

3) patton-cemetery-belle-gunness-victims.mp4

Some of Belle Gunness's victims are buried right here in La Porte 🕯️ Patton Cemetery on Rumely Street dates to 1841 and holds some of the oldest graves in the area. Her teenage foster daughter, Jennie Olson, rests here, and so does Andrew Helgelien, the farmer who answered her ad. Visitors describe shadows between the stones, voices with no one there, and a heavy sadness near her victims. Please visit in daylight and keep it respectful; families still visit these graves. 4) orr-lake-road-hangmans-road.mp4

Some people in La Porte call Orr Lake Road "Hangman's Road" 🚗 The story goes, a couple's car died out there one night. He went for help and told her to lock the doors. All night she heard a slow scraping across the roof... There's no record it ever happened. Folklorists call it "The Boyfriend's Death," a legend told across Indiana for generations. Double-tap ❤️ if you heard it growing up. People live on Orr Lake Road, so if you drive it, keep rolling.

5) belle-gunness-part-6-the-dna-test.mp4

Part 6: Almost a hundred years after the fire, scientists dug up the headless woman 🧬 In 1908 she'd been buried near Chicago, at Forest Home Cemetery, in the plot of Belle's first husband. In November 2007, University of Indianapolis researchers exhumed her and tried to match her DNA to an envelope Belle had mailed to a suitor, but the old sample couldn't be properly tested. The answer is still buried. In April 2008, descendants of her victims finally gave their graves headstones. Double-tap ❤️ to remember them.
